If you’re in this position, act quickly and document everything; how long water sits, how deep it goes, and what materials are involved make the greatest difference in final cost.

Contents: pack-out, cleaning and what you can claim

If water gets into your Lehigh home, the contents—the stuff inside—matter just as much as your floors and walls. Most insurance claims shortchange this side, usually because homeowners don’t have a complete inventory before work starts. Pack-out is a service where a crew photographs, inventories, and moves your belongings off-site for cleaning, drying, and safe storage. Before anything moves, insist on documentation: clear photos and a list of every salvaged item, with notations for what’s too far gone.

Your insurance policy treats the structure (actual house) and contents separately. Upholstered furniture exposed to sewage (Category 3) is almost never salvageable; so are mattresses, pillows, and low-end particleboard that falls apart when wet. Rugs, hardwood furniture, electronics, and area rugs often can be dried and restored. Porous items stored in a typical Lehigh masonry basement—books, textiles—rarely survive if soaked for long. The more detail and photos you have, the more you can claim, and the process for cleaning is straightforward if handled by pros familiar with both cleaning and insurance claim requirements. Remember, accurate records mean a fairer settlement when your adjuster arrives.

Equipment, monitoring and drying standards

Efficient drying in Lehigh’s climate isn't about having more loud fans in a room—it's about documenting results. Restoration starts with moisture mapping: using meters to record where and how much water is present, sometimes behind old plaster walls or within masonry. Accept nothing less than daily meter readings, logged in writing. This record is your proof that drying was done right.

Air movers circulate air across wet surfaces, increasing evaporation. LGR (low grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers then pull moisture out of the air, bringing humidity down fast. In Lehigh County’s 6,100 heating degree days and cold winters, outdoor air often can’t be used to help dry—machines do the heavy lifting. Equipment must run day and night, sometimes for 3–5 days. Shutting it off at night slows progress, so you’ll pay more and risk mold. Every reputable crew will visit daily, read all areas, and record progress toward the “drying goal”—a measured, written moisture content matching undamaged areas or industry standard, not a gut feeling.

The real test: ask for the daily moisture logs. If a company can’t hand these over, they weren’t monitoring properly. Don’t be fooled by noise and busywork; in Lehigh, drying is a controlled process, not a guessing game.

Regulation, certification and verification in Lehigh

Pennsylvania does not license restoration or mold remediation contractors—there is no state-issued trade licence in these fields. This means seeing a business card or logo tells you nothing about actual qualifications. The de facto standard in Lehigh and across the state is IICRC certification. Always ask for the lead technician’s IICRC S500 or S520 card and verify it matches the crew on site.

Permits are only required in Bethlehem and some townships if you move from drying to reconstruction—such as drywall replacement after a water loss. Simple extraction and drying, particularly in Easton, do not trigger building permits. For any restoration contract over $500, the firm must hold a Home Improvement Contractor registration from the Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General. Homeowners are guaranteed a written contract and a 3-business-day right of cancellation.

Always ask for proof of current general liability and workers’ compensation coverage. Verification is public through the WCAIS system for workers’ comp. Get everything in writing before equipment crosses your threshold: crew qualifications, contract, and insurance evidence.

Flooding vs plumbing failure in Lehigh

The Lehigh River and Delaware River corridors make local flooding a known risk in Northampton County—not just a “hundred-year” event. If your address is in a recognized floodplain, your regular homeowners insurance policy will not cover overbank or storm-driven flooding. Flood insurance must be purchased separately, and most policies have a 30-day waiting period before they take effect. That means if you try to buy coverage when a big storm is forecasted, it's already too late.

Restoration crews in Lehigh see flooded basements from both sources. A finished basement soaked by the Lehigh River looks much like one flooded by a burst washing machine supply line—but an insurance adjuster will treat them as worlds apart. Flood claims fall under your FEMA policy or private flood insurance, not your ordinary home insurance. Always double-check whether your coverage is for true flood (water from outside) or just water damage due to plumbing failure (inside-the-envelope events).

If you live along the Lehigh or Delaware River, know your zone and check every year at policy renewal. Being outside the mapped floodplain does not mean zero risk—but it does mean an adjuster may deny a “flood” claim for groundwater or stormwater backup. Document every event and save correspondence with your insurer for clarity later.

Sewage backup: a different job entirely

Sewage backups in Lehigh—especially in older areas of Easton—are not just a smelly water loss. Backups mean high-level contamination requiring full biohazard protocol. Crews enter in full PPE, establish containment barriers, and remove affected porous materials: carpets, upholstered furniture, and insulation get bagged and disposed unimpressed by how clean something “looks.” No amount of surface cleaning makes a mattress or pressed-wood shelving safe after a sewage exposure.

Everything remaining gets thoroughly disinfected and deodorized before rebuilding. Occupants, especially children or the immune-compromised, should not be present during cleanup. Sanitary sewer backups are a recurring problem here—Easton’s aging infrastructure and periodic backup events managed by the Easton Suburban Water Authority make this a real risk, not a one-off event. Some homes install backflow prevention valves, though cost-sharing from the municipality is not in effect locally. Service rates for sewage cleanup—true Category 3—run $2,500–$12,000. Always make sure your contractor has handled this specific hazard before, not just “water damage,” as many will quote both but only a few follow real biohazard protocols.

When water damage becomes a mold problem

The clock starts the moment water hits a surface in your Lehigh home. Mold colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours when conditions are right—especially in the basements common to old Bethlehem and Easton rowhomes. In summer, high humidity and less ventilation speed this up. First-day response usually means clean water extraction and in-place drying. Wait past 48 hours, and selective demolition comes into play: wet baseboards, drywall, and maybe old plaster must be removed for the area to dry at all.

Once mold is visible or musty odors linger, it’s no longer just a drying job. Now you’re looking at containment, HEPA air filtration, and after abatement, a clearance test—independent confirmation, in writing, that spore counts are at or below outside levels. Mold remediation costs in Lehigh start at $1,500–$10,500, depending on area and contamination. If caught within the first day, standard water extraction and drying ($1,200–$7,500) may prevent mold entirely. Delay turns a drying job into a full remediation with added testing—and costs.

Always request a written clearance report. Without it, you have no record that mold was properly removed, and a future buyer or insurer may question what was actually done.

Frequently asked questions — water damage restoration in Lehigh

How fast do restoration companies respond in Lehigh? Restoration companies in Lehigh, especially those based in Bethlehem and Easton, typically offer 24/7 emergency response. For active pipe breaks or storm flooding, expect a crew on site within two to four hours, even overnight. Dense coverage by regional firms like SERVPRO of Easton, Bethlehem and Whitehall allows faster dispatch than more rural counties. However, severe storm events or widespread river flooding along the Lehigh or Delaware River can extend response times when many homes are affected at once. Most reputable firms will at least provide initial water extraction and install emergency drying equipment on the first visit—often running $1,200–$7,500 for a Category 1 event. Always document any delay and start your insurance claim promptly, as the time water sits in your home directly impacts both cost and the complexity of restoration.
Why is water damage priced per square foot? Water damage restoration in Lehigh is typically priced per square foot because larger areas require more equipment, more labor, and longer monitoring. For example, drying 1,500 sq ft of original hardwood in a Bethlehem colonial involves more air movers, dehumidifiers, and sometimes more teardown than a smaller, modern bathroom leak. Square footage also dictates material and disposal costs, especially in masonry basements or homes with lath and plaster construction common to Easton and Bethlehem. Most companies will measure affected areas, then multiply by the per-foot rate to give a project total—Category 1 water removal starts at $1,200–$7,500. This approach makes the price fairer and more predictable for both owner and contractor, and is standard practice throughout Northampton County.
What causes most water damage in Lehigh homes? Most water damage losses in Lehigh result from aging plumbing in Bethlehem and Easton's pre-1940s housing, combined with finished basements and seasonal freeze-thaw cycles. Old galvanized pipes, failed supply lines, and overworked sump pumps—especially after heavy rains near the Lehigh and Delaware River corridors—top the list. While overbank flooding is a well-known risk, day-to-day claims more often involve pipe failures behind plaster, washing machine or water heater leaks, and seepage into full masonry basements. These events commonly require water extraction and emergency drying, costing between $1,200–$7,500, depending on the scope and response time. Regular plumbing maintenance and monitoring sump performance can prevent many of these headaches.
Does insurance cover mold after water damage? In Pennsylvania and Northampton County, most standard homeowner policies exclude mold as a covered peril unless it is the result of a "sudden and accidental" water loss that you report immediately and mitigate fast. If a pipe bursts and you initiate water extraction and drying right away, mold remediation may be covered up to the policy’s “mold sublimit” (usually low: $5,000 to $10,000). If the insurer determines mold growth resulted from long-term neglect or an unresolved moisture issue—common in older Easton and Bethlehem basements—coverage is typically denied. Mold remediation with proper containment and clearance testing can run $1,500–$10,500 in Lehigh. Always check your policy language and notify your carrier as soon as water damage occurs to preserve coverage.
What is a clearance test and do I need one? A clearance test is an independent air quality test performed after mold remediation to verify that the affected area is safe and spore counts are at or below outside (background) levels. In Lehigh, where older homes and basements are common, clearance testing is highly recommended—especially if you ever plan to sell or refinance. The process involves taking air or surface samples, usually by a third party, and comparing results to baseline levels. Good contractors will include clearance testing in your contract if you request it. Mold remediation projects above $1,500–$10,500 should always end with a written clearance report. Without it, there’s no proof that remediation was complete or effective, which can create problems with insurance or future property transactions.
What if the insurer's estimate is lower than the contractor's? In Lehigh, it’s common for an insurer’s first estimate to be lower than the contractor’s—especially if the adjuster uses generic pricing or doesn’t see hidden issues typical in Bethlehem and Easton’s older homes (like plaster or uninsulated masonry). Don’t panic. Your contractor should be able to back up their scope and costs with written moisture logs, photographs, and supporting measurements. Detailed documentation—especially logs showing equipment runtime and square footage—helps support your claim. Category 1 mitigation in this market ranges from $1,200–$7,500. If the gap remains, ask the contractor to negotiate directly with your insurer using their estimates and job documentation. If the claim involves special materials or unforeseen mold, let the adjuster know before authorizing any repair changes.
Is bleach effective against mold? Bleach is not recommended for mold remediation in Lehigh or anywhere else. While bleach can lighten mold stains on hard surfaces, it does not kill mold roots inside porous materials like drywall, wood framing, or plaster—especially common in older Bethlehem and Easton homes. EPA and IICRC guidelines call for physical removal of mold-contaminated materials, proper containment, and HEPA filtration. Professional remediation, falling in the $1,500–$10,500 band for this area, employs antimicrobial agents only as a final step after removal. Homeowners should avoid bleach and trust an IICRC-certified contractor who uses both physical and chemical methods—and provides clearance testing to verify completion.
What does mold remediation cost in Lehigh? The cost of mold remediation with containment in Lehigh typically runs between $1,500–$10,500. The price depends on where the mold is, how much area is affected, the construction type (old lath-and-plaster or new drywall), and whether the job requires selective demolition. Typical jobs in older Bethlehem rowhomes trend around the $6,000 mark. This includes setting up containment, deploying HEPA air scrubbers, removal of contaminated materials, treatment with antimicrobials, and clearance testing. If caught quickly—within 48 hours of a water event—simple drying or removal under $1,200–$7,500 may be enough, but delays usually raise the price into full remediation territory. Always request a detailed proposal and ask for moisture and clearance test documentation.
Does wet insulation have to be replaced? In most cases in Lehigh, wet batt insulation (fiberglass or cellulose) inside walls, ceilings, or crawlspaces does need to be replaced. These materials hold moisture, dry slowly, and become a breeding ground for mold if not removed promptly. In older Bethlehem and Easton homes, retrofitted insulation in exterior walls and basements often traps water behind plaster, making effective drying impossible without opening up. Professional contractors will check inaccessible cavities with moisture meters and recommend removal if readings stay elevated. The removal and replacement costs add to your job—mold remediation jobs involving insulation run toward $1,500–$10,500. On the other hand, rigid foam board or spray foam in unfinished spaces may sometimes be dried and salvaged if addressed immediately.
Does Pennsylvania license mold remediation contractors? Pennsylvania does not have a state-issued license for mold remediation contractors, including in Northampton County and Lehigh Valley. There is no official state registry or test. Any firm advertising “licensed mold removal” is referring to other business or home improvement registrations. In this state, the insurer-expected standard is certification to IICRC S520 (mold) and S500 (water damage), which are industry guidelines recognizing a firm’s education and protocol compliance. Always request to see the contractor’s certification and check for a Home Improvement Contractor registration for any contract over $500. Mold remediation projects in the Lehigh area typically fall into the $1,500–$10,500 range—don’t overpay just because a company claims special “licensure.”
